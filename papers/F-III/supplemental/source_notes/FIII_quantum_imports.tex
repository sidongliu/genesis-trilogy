\section{Imported quantum continuation}\label{app:quantum_imports}
\subsection{A renewed receiver in the next retained window}
\label{subsec:continuation}

A decision rule can jump at a posterior threshold without making the
physical history map discontinuous. If the rule is fixed, it acts on a
record already present in the state. This observation removes one
unnecessary continuity assumption from quantum returns.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed history control and its physical source]
\label{prop:continuation}
Let the complete classical--quantum history, including held content
and old references, be retained. An acquisition instrument followed
by a fixed, legal record-controlled channel is nonexpansive in half
trace distance. Thus $N$ such rounds with uniform complete-instrument
defects $\epsilon_n$ have error at most $d_0+\sum_{n<N}\epsilon_n$,
irrespective of discontinuities in the controller's decision rule.

For source transport, let normalized purifications $\psi,\varphi$
include the material, controller, clock and every reference. Assume
$\|\psi-\varphi\|\le e$ and
$\|H_F\psi\|,\|H_F\varphi\|\le M_F$, and continue both under the
same positive quantum-material Hamiltonian of Q-RAIF~C.
For its smooth interacting-window field-source form $O_{\rm int}$
with
\[
 \|(H_F+\Lambda)^{-1/2}O_{\rm int}
                      (H_F+\Lambda)^{-1/2}\|\le C_{\rm int},
 \qquad \Lambda>0,
\]
the difference of actual next-window sources is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nextsource}
 2C_{\rm int}\sqrt{\Lambda e^2+2M_Fe}\sqrt{\Lambda+M_F}.
\end{equation}
The two inputs must each meet the form domain and rms bound.
A later bounded record or action loss in $[0,1]$ differs by at most $e$.
No conditioning on a rare or successful branch is used.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An instrument maps $\rho$ to
$\bigoplus_r\mathcal I_r(\rho)$, with unnormalized branches and
$\sum_r\mathcal I_r$ trace preserving. Applying a trace-preserving
channel to each block gives another CPTP map on the complete history.
Tensor it with the identity on old references. Trace-norm
contractivity and the triangle inequality give the first assertion
and its finite error sum. A coherent controller instead has one
fixed channel on its full quantum register; the same argument applies.

For the source assertion, use Q-RAIF~C's interacting-window form,
with the same $K$, vacuum subtraction, smooth profiles and compact
space--time test. Set $A=H_F+\Lambda$.
The form difference is bounded by
$C_{\rm int}\|A^{1/2}(\psi-\varphi)\|
(\|A^{1/2}\psi\|+\|A^{1/2}\varphi\|)$.
Cauchy--Schwarz and
$\|H_F(\psi-\varphi)\|\le2M_F$ give
$\|A^{1/2}(\psi-\varphi)\|^2\le\Lambda e^2+2M_Fe$;
each remaining norm is at most $\sqrt{\Lambda+M_F}$.
This proves~\eqref{eq:nextsource}. Common unitary evolution preserves
the vector error; a later complete instrument contracts half trace
distance, which is at most $e$ for these pure joint states. Bounded
losses obey the same bound.
\end{proof}

The channel inequality is standard. Its role here is to place
history-dependent decisions, old references and the physical source
in one comparison. It does not make a nonlinear semiclassical
mean-action map contractive. Nor does it bound unbounded controller
stress from a bounded-loss test.

\begin{corollary}[Finite renewal remains usable without a new clock]
\label{cor:usable-renewal}
Use Q-RAIF~C's receiver-renewal family with fixed $A>0$,
$p\in(0,1/2)$, even clock length $m$ and coupling $J=A/m$.
The first edge is a true receiver--bank SWAP, later edges are
identities, and the material force is supported at $j\ge m/2$.
Keep the initially correlated old receiver, held content, preparation
purifiers and complete cursor. At
$t_R=(m/A)\arcsin\sqrt p$, its actual purification differs from the
completed-SWAP comparison on the same untrimmed cursor by at most
\[
 b_m=2(1-p)^{m/2}
 +\frac{m\arcsin\sqrt p}{A}
   \left[L_\Delta\sqrt M e^{-mD/4}
                       +c_\Delta e^{-mD/2}\right],
 \qquad D=D_{\rm Ber}(1/2\|p)>0 .
\]
Continue those two states under that same interacting Hamiltonian,
without resetting or translating the cursor. On any fixed subsequent
window satisfying the smooth-source conditions, bounded next-task
errors tend to zero with $b_m$, and the field-source difference tends
to zero by~\eqref{eq:nextsource} with $e=b_m$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The stated full-purification bound is the renewal estimate of
Q-RAIF~C, not a marginal-only reset. Its cell energies and
force/counterterm norms are fixed, and the history-clock norm is at
most $2A$. The actual physical rms is uniformly bounded by its
positive-Hamiltonian certificate. The comparison applies a completed
SWAP to the old receiver and independent Gibbs bank, leaving the
material input unchanged; the SWAP commutes with the two cell
energies. Its material rms and its total-energy graph norm are
therefore bounded independently of $m$ as well. The same certificate
bounds its continued physical rms. The smooth source constants depend
on the fixed subsequent window, $K$, profiles and coupling norms,
not on the cursor site count. Proposition~\ref{prop:continuation}
now applies, and $b_m\to0$.
\end{proof}

The comparison cursor in this corollary is not concentrated in a
ready site. To execute another prescribed round one needs an actual
later program on that cursor. Q-RAIF~C's finite-sequence construction
provides it, with a different clock tradeoff.

\begin{corollary}[Several returns with the same body and history]
\label{cor:installed-returns}
Fix a finite number of rounds, a complete Gaussian material $K$,
smooth linear probe/record forces, and finite reversible decisions
using only the actual retained records. Suppose the data gates
commute with their nonnegative bare energy; matched-cell swaps and
degenerate logic are permitted. Initially supply the finite bank,
workspace and their preparation references.
Then one positive autonomous Hamiltonian implements the successive
readings, decisions, forces and receiver replacements to arbitrarily
small specified joint error at any fixed finite set of checkpoints
away from the comparison gate times. It retains the same body,
old references, spent cells and untrimmed cursor. The complete
sequence ends at $T=\pi/(2J)$; clock energy and dimension grow with
precision. Its material rms has an $m$-independent bound, so the
same-$H_m$ source comparisons of~\eqref{eq:nextsource} remain valid.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Place all rounds on the single threshold path of Q-RAIF~C's
finite-sequence proposition. Its prefix-gauge Duhamel error is
$O(m^{-1/2})$; wrong-prefix probabilities at checkpoints with a
nonzero threshold margin decay exponentially. Its direct material
commutator estimate bounds the rms independently of clock size.
A swap transfers the old receiver and its correlations into the
next spent bank, while data gates preserve the material graph domain.
Thus each round ends in the actual input domain of the next, with
no replacement clock. The retained-history and source estimates
above apply to this common process. A finite history-dependent
decision table is compiled reversibly with its inputs and garbage
retained; it never needs the inaccessible true fault as an input.
\end{proof}

The clock has $m+1$ states, initial mean $mJ$ and norm $2mJ$.
All banks, old records and arithmetic storage are charged. This
finite construction supplies an installed next round; fixed-capacity
operation as $N\to\infty$ and the renormalized total apparatus source
remain additional physical requirements. In particular the material
source certificate is not a quantum JT equation.
