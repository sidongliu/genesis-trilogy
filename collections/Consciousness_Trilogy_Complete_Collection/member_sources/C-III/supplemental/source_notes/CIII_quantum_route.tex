Q-RAIF~C gives an autonomous finite implementation route
\cite{QRAIFC}: reversibly compile the finite decision circuit into
record-controlled gates on a positive history clock. The pilot meters
copy only commuting pointer values; the repair flips the held coordinate.
The same physical repair program can control a two-port thermal
exchange, retaining both streams and their correlations. That adds an
actual source and its reservoir ledger, rather than an emission keyed
to the scorer's knowledge of whether repair was correct. Source modes,
noise ancillas, primitive gates and clock initialization remain supplied.
An explicit first-interval Hamiltonian contains all three body bits,
three physical meters and both thermal cells on a nine-state clock.
Its actual meter word controls repair and exchange, giving restoration
failure $.02891$ and cold-cell energy gain $.00761031$. All initial
purification references are retained. This realizes one diagnostic
return under a single clock. The same construction extends to every
specified finite horizon by preallocating fault ancillas, meters, thermal
pairs and reversible memory. The single completed Hamiltonian then
realizes the entire joint law without re-preparing the held body.
For three intervals, exact branch enumeration gives $.44635607$
expected repairs and $.02506944$ total cold-cell gain. The cost grows
with the horizon; this does not identify the repeated stochastic
simulation with a fixed-capacity cyclic thermal apparatus or with
equally spaced proper-time samples.
The continued functional benefit makes causal return experimentally
consequential; independent evidence is still needed for experience.
