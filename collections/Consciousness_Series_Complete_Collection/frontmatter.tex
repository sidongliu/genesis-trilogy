\section*{Introduction}
% COLLECTION INTRODUCTION START

The six papers combine the realization questions of Foundation with the geometric and self-description questions of Consciousness. The first trilogy addresses selection, shared manifestation and physical return; the second asks what the associated modular geometry and finite self-models explain about a first-person organization.

The two trilogies meet through declared interfaces. The stable algebra selected by noise need not itself be a Cut on the ambient Hilbert space. A shared structure need not carry a common state. A returned quantum receiver need not constitute a complete cyclic thermal apparatus. Carrying these distinctions allows the genuine positive connections to support the broader explanatory proposal.

The accompanying corpus also includes the overview, the three Q-RAIF companion papers, Brain Anchor and four Imprint papers. Brain Anchor tests learned access, damage and repair on a continuing body. Imprint distinguishes carrying a trace, reading its content and using self-reading to choose later operations. These are independent contributions; they are not absorbed into Foundation III's finite scalar/JT return.

\section*{Reading map}
\begin{description}
\item[F-I] \textit{Foundation I: From Source to Manifestation. Accessible Algebras from a Source, Its Noise and a Readable Seed}. A source, its noise and a readable seed define an accessible hull. Selection and finite positive-energy preparation require their declared dynamical permissions.
\item[F-II] \textit{Foundation II: Shared Manifestation. Shared Structure, Public Calibration and Different Histories}. Shared structure does not impose identical states or reliable common beliefs. Public references distinguish agreement, correctness and shared error.
\item[F-III] \textit{Foundation III: The Perfuming Loop. Held Content, Learned Frames and Physical Return}. A return preserves old content while learned frames, physical action and source response change what can be read next. Receiver renewal is not renewal of an entire apparatus.
\item[C-I] \textit{Geometry Relative to a Cut: Selection, Generation and Modular Inseparability}. A local seed generates a standard Cut under the stated modular dynamics. Finite stress-flow tasks have a paid linear-current realization in the neutral carrier; maintaining that access under selecting noise remains a separate question.
\item[C-II] \textit{Higher-Dimensional Consistency of Geometry and Modular Flow: Physical Source Identification and a Second-Order Extension}. Ball data test geometric consistency using a physical source. The second-order extension retains extrinsic terms and the preparation and boundary data that change with geometry.
\item[C-III] \textit{Finite Self-Models and the Explanatory Gap: What a Continuing Observer Can Know About Itself}. Self-description has task-specific limits. Finite records, drift and chosen permissions determine risk; a proposed bridge to experience requires its own intervention tests.
\end{description}
