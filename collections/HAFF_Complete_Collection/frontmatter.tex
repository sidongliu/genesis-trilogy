\section*{Introduction}
% COLLECTION INTRODUCTION START

Which relations constitute a world that an apparatus can read and sustain? The HAFF papers approach this question through channels, couplings, records and interventions. They distinguish a reconstructed description from a change in the matter that carries it. The gravitational proposal is therefore tested twice: through consistency of geometric data and through sources produced by the interactions that change access.

Read A and B together to establish the operational objects, then E for their measurement realization. C and F develop agency and temporal direction. D addresses the source and geometry interfaces; G returns those interfaces to revisable organization. The postscript discusses what persistence and participation mean for a finite system.



\section*{Reading map}
\begin{description}
\item[HAFF-A] \textit{Physical Access and Correlation Geometry: The Holographic Alaya–Field Framework}. Physical channels and their record laws distinguish operational access from an abstract algebra. One initial preparation and fixed reconstruction rule can give non-isometric correlation geometries after different physical reductions.
\item[HAFF-B] \textit{Coupling, Records, and Stable Relations: Formation, Protected Content and the Lifetime of a Geometry}. Couplings select what survives. Protected content, slowly decaying relations and physically acquired records have distinct roles in maintaining a description.
\item[HAFF-C] \textit{Causation, Agency, and Existence: Historical Control and Maintained Intervention}. Agency is tested by interventions allowed by a retained history, including the maintenance needed for those interventions to remain available.
\item[HAFF-D] \textit{Selection, Maintenance and Gravitational Response: Modular Constraints and a Physical Return}. Selection reaches gravitational response through an actual source. The paper joins finite physical returns to modular constraints and develops a common-action conjecture for the missing general mechanism.
\item[HAFF-E] \textit{Measurement as Accessibility: Measurement Permissions, Stable Records and Public Readout}. An implementable measurement, a stable pointer and a public record need not coincide. Explicit permissions and instruments determine their connections.
\item[HAFF-F] \textit{Records, Dissipation and Intervention: Conditions for a Temporal Direction}. A temporal direction requires an appropriate record and dissipation account. Causal order, entropy production and an agent's stored history are not interchangeable.
\item[HAFF-G] \textit{Internal Selection and Revisable Organization: How History Changes Permissions and Physical Sources}. History can revise the operations a system selects. A target operation remains distinct from its larger executor; physical programming and source changes have costs.
\item[HAFF-PS] \textit{Postscript: Persistence, Participation and an Unfinished Self}. Persistence and participation are interpreted through the finite constructions, without equating a useful self-model with a complete self-description.
\end{description}
