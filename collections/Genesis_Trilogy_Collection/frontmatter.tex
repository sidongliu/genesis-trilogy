\section*{Introduction}
% COLLECTION INTRODUCTION START

The central question is how a readable world, a physical interface and an agent able to revise its own use of that interface can form in one process. HAFF develops the world side, Q-RAIF the physical interface, and T-DOME the generation and upkeep of the subject's functional organization. None of the three can be replaced by the most tractable model in another.

The connections have concrete consequences. In a finite semiclassical mean-action scalar/JT realization, the same acquired record protects held quantum content and changes the apparatus's source. At fixed initial mass and geometry, the resulting response enters a later X-basis reading of the old probe into a spent cell, which remains in the continuing mechanical interaction. The task and return share one finite window, cursor and complete reference account. Other models establish shared structure and the value of self-reading. They retain their own physical inputs; the sustained quantum and thermal realization is a stronger problem than this finite return.

The accompanying corpus also includes the overview, the three Q-RAIF companion papers, Brain Anchor and four Imprint papers. Brain Anchor tests learned access, damage and repair on a continuing body. Imprint distinguishes carrying a trace, reading its content and using self-reading to choose later operations. These are independent contributions; they are not absorbed into Foundation III's finite scalar/JT return.

\section*{Reading map}
\begin{description}
\item[HAFF-A] \textit{Physical Access and Correlation Geometry: The Holographic Alaya–Field Framework}. Physical channels and their record laws distinguish operational access from an abstract algebra. One initial preparation and fixed reconstruction rule can give non-isometric correlation geometries after different physical reductions.
\item[HAFF-B] \textit{Coupling, Records, and Stable Relations: Formation, Protected Content and the Lifetime of a Geometry}. Couplings select what survives. Protected content, slowly decaying relations and physically acquired records have distinct roles in maintaining a description.
\item[HAFF-C] \textit{Causation, Agency, and Existence: Historical Control and Maintained Intervention}. Agency is tested by interventions allowed by a retained history, including the maintenance needed for those interventions to remain available.
\item[HAFF-D] \textit{Selection, Maintenance and Gravitational Response: Modular Constraints and a Physical Return}. Selection reaches gravitational response through an actual source. The paper joins finite physical returns to modular constraints and develops a common-action conjecture for the missing general mechanism.
\item[HAFF-E] \textit{Measurement as Accessibility: Measurement Permissions, Stable Records and Public Readout}. An implementable measurement, a stable pointer and a public record need not coincide. Explicit permissions and instruments determine their connections.
\item[HAFF-F] \textit{Records, Dissipation and Intervention: Conditions for a Temporal Direction}. A temporal direction requires an appropriate record and dissipation account. Causal order, entropy production and an agent's stored history are not interchangeable.
\item[HAFF-G] \textit{Internal Selection and Revisable Organization: How History Changes Permissions and Physical Sources}. History can revise the operations a system selects. A target operation remains distinct from its larger executor; physical programming and source changes have costs.
\item[HAFF-PS] \textit{Postscript: Persistence, Participation and an Unfinished Self}. Persistence and participation are interpreted through the finite constructions, without equating a useful self-model with a complete self-description.
\item[QR-A] \textit{Lorentzian Read-out from Local Unitary Propagation: Clifford Relations, Causal Cones and Spectral Mass}. Local unitary propagation produces per-species Clifford readout and causal cones. Speed alignment requires divergent accumulated coupling in the mediator model, or decay of relative-speed perturbations near a supplied fixed point; connectivity alone is insufficient.
\item[QR-B] \textit{Thermodynamic Stability Constraints on Persistent Open Quantum Subsystems: Control Algebra, Physical Actuation and Energy Cost}. Persistent control is realized by physical couplings. Protection, calibration, active recovery and field energy are compared under explicit collection and reference permissions.
\item[QR-C] \textit{Retained Quantum History, Physical Sources and Receiver Renewal in Q-RAIF}. One autonomous path performs finite repeated readings and receiver updates while retaining the body and history. Local linear-current control also implements neutral-sector stress flows; clock work and the limits of total-source matching are explicit.
\item[TD-I] \textit{Memory as a Usable Resource: Correlations, Records and Complete Endpoint Accounting}. The work value of records depends on their physical readout and retained side information. The endpoint account keeps input purity and the outputs of correction visible.
\item[TD-II] \textit{Reference Frames Selected by History: The Capability and Cost of a Bounded Agent's Self-Frame}. An agent's usable frame is selected by its actual history. Oriented records and legal readouts determine what it can calibrate and control.
\item[TD-III] \textit{Keeping a Self-Model Calibrated: Drift Detection, the Price of Keeping Up, and Reliability in a Closed Loop}. Drift makes continued self-calibration a task. Retained histories determine reference requests, paid maintenance and action authority on one continuing body.
\end{description}
