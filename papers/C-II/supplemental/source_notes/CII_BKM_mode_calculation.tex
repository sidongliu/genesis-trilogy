\section{Regularity of the quartic entropy law}\label{app:bkm}

The continuum regularity issue can be exposed in the actual free-field mode model.
Consider a charged oscillator mode with modular thermal parameter \(b>0\),
\(\rho_b=(1-e^{-b})e^{-bN}\), and a displacement \(D(\theta\alpha)\).
The \(U(1)\) average removes off-diagonal number components.
Writing \(z=e^{-b}\), its order-\(\theta^2\) diagonal coefficient is
\[
\chi_n=|\alpha|^2p_n\bigl[(1-z)^2n/z-(1-z)\bigr],
\qquad p_n=(1-z)z^n .
\]
The geometric distribution has mean \(z/(1-z)\) and variance \(z/(1-z)^2\), hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modebkm}
g_{\mathrm{BKM},\rho_b}(\chi,\chi)
=\sum_n\frac{\chi_n^2}{p_n}
=|\alpha|^4\frac{(1-z)^2}{z}
=4|\alpha|^4\sinh^2(b/2).
\end{equation}
For finitely many independent charged modes, measurement of their number occupations
is an invariant commuting readout. Its diagonal BKM norm is the sum of these terms;
cross terms vanish because each score has zero mean. This readout gives a necessary
regularity test on any regulated quartic coefficient of the full invariant algebra.
It also shows the relevant obstruction: high modular frequencies carry exponential
weights, whereas finite modular energy controls only \(\sum_j b_j|\alpha_j|^2\)
in this thermal mode representation.
The diagonal test is not a sufficient condition for the full BKM norm, which can include
invariant off-diagonal mode coherences.

Those coherences can also be computed in a regulated charge sector. For finitely many
independent modes of the same charge, put
\(\rho=\bigotimes_j(1-z_j)z_j^{N_j}\) and
\(D(\theta\alpha)=\exp[\theta\sum_j(\alpha_ja_j^\dagger-\overline\alpha_ja_j)]\).
Average over the common \(U(1)\) phase. The full invariant order-\(\theta^2\)
coefficient has BKM norm
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multimodebkm}
g_{\mathrm{BKM},\rho}(\chi,\chi)
=\sum_{j,l}|\alpha_j|^2|\alpha_l|^2
 (1-z_j)(1-z_l)\frac{\log z_j-\log z_l}{z_j-z_l},
\end{equation}
where the coincident value is \((1-z_j)^2/z_j\).
To see the off-diagonal term, let \(m=n+e_j-e_l\), \(j\ne l\).
Expanding the double commutator of the displacement generator gives
\[
\chi_{mn}=\alpha_j\overline\alpha_l
 \sqrt{(n_j+1)n_l}\,p_n\frac{(1-z_j)(1-z_l)}{z_l}.
\]
Here \(p_m/p_n=z_j/z_l\). Substitution into the spectral BKM formula and averaging
\((n_j+1)n_l\), whose mean is \(z_l/[(1-z_j)(1-z_l)]\), gives the
ordered \((j,l)\) term in~\eqref{eq:multimodebkm}. The diagonal terms are
\eqref{eq:modebkm}; their cross terms vanish.

The mean-value theorem gives the bound
\[
g_{\mathrm{BKM},\rho}(\chi,\chi)
\le 2\Big(\sum_j|\alpha_j|^2\Big)
       \Big(\sum_j e^{b_j}|\alpha_j|^2\Big),\qquad b_j=-\log z_j.
\]
An exponential modular-frequency moment is therefore a concrete sufficient condition
for a bounded BKM tangent norm in this charge sector, uniformly in finite mode
approximations.

For charges \(q_j=\pm1\), the complex scalar also has neutral pair creation between
oppositely charged modes. The same calculation now uses \(m=n+e_j+e_l\) and gives
\(\chi_{mn}=\alpha_j\alpha_l\sqrt{(n_j+1)(n_l+1)}p_n(1-z_j)(1-z_l)\).
The full mode coefficient is the same-charge sum in~\eqref{eq:multimodebkm} plus
\[
\sum_{q_j=-q_l}|\alpha_j|^2|\alpha_l|^2
 (1-z_j)(1-z_l)\frac{b_j+b_l}{1-z_jz_l}.
\]
The ordered sum counts creation and annihilation matrix elements. Since
\((1-z_j)(1-z_l)\le1-z_jz_l\), its kernel is at most \(b_j+b_l\).
Thus, writing \(A=\sum_j|\alpha_j|^2\), \(E=\sum_je^{b_j}|\alpha_j|^2\), and
\(L=\sum_jb_j|\alpha_j|^2\), a sufficient bound for the entire regulated complex-field
tangent is \(g_{\mathrm{BKM}}\le2A(E+L)\).
This is a smearing criterion for the tangent coefficient, rather than a proof of a
uniform entropy expansion. It separates the exponential same-charge obstruction
from the neutral-pair terms, which need only the modular-energy moment.

The free scalar on a ball has a continuous modular spectrum rather than a trace-class
density matrix. To carry the quartic law to that net one must control the invariant
two-particle displacement coefficient in its modular BKM space and the entropy remainder
uniformly as mode, occupation and volume regulators are removed. No bound on those
exponential modular-frequency moments has been derived here from compactly supported
smooth spacetime smearing. Establishing this estimate, or identifying a smearing for which it fails, remains
a specific free-field problem. The exact strict inequality in part~(a) does not depend on it.
