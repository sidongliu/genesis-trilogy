\section*{Introduction}
% COLLECTION INTRODUCTION START

These papers ask how geometry and the organization of a reader constrain one another. A Cut has a precise modular meaning within its stated class. Higher-dimensional ball data test geometric consistency against a prepared physical source. A finite self-model faces limits that depend on its permissions, records and drift.

The mathematical structures and the experience question retain different proof obligations. A successful self-calibration task is evidence for functional reflection, not a theorem of subjective experience. The experience bridge is kept as an explicit hypothesis with possible interventions rather than removed from the theory or hidden inside the definition of a Cut.



\section*{Reading map}
\begin{description}
\item[C-I] \textit{Geometry Relative to a Cut: Selection, Generation and Modular Inseparability}. A local seed generates a standard Cut under the stated modular dynamics. Finite stress-flow tasks have a paid linear-current realization in the neutral carrier; maintaining that access under selecting noise remains a separate question.
\item[C-II] \textit{Higher-Dimensional Consistency of Geometry and Modular Flow: Physical Source Identification and a Second-Order Extension}. Ball data test geometric consistency using a physical source. The second-order extension retains extrinsic terms and the preparation and boundary data that change with geometry.
\item[C-III] \textit{Finite Self-Models and the Explanatory Gap: What a Continuing Observer Can Know About Itself}. Self-description has task-specific limits. Finite records, drift and chosen permissions determine risk; a proposed bridge to experience requires its own intervention tests.
\end{description}
